From the cavern floor—two kilometers of obsidian platform stretching away like a black ocean—the perspective tilts upward to capture the Cobalt Fabrication Labs, a three-tiered industrial leviathan carved into crater wall with fastidious brutality. Eight hundred vertical meters of geometric repetition: two hundred rectangular voids, each one hundred by eighty meters, arranged in calculated rows across Levels 36 through 38, their entrances framed in brass that once gleamed but now bears decades of thermal abuse. The metal frames hold relief patterns—gear assemblies interlocking with molecular lattices, crystalline structures suggesting metallurgical heritage—but heat has warped the brass, and accumulated metal particulate has cloaked the scrollwork in grimy rainbow-oil patina, beauty sacrificed to urgency and function. Beyond the complex perimeter, three hundred meters distant, the extraction zone's ore processors and Fracturing Plant cooling towers rise like secondary mountains, exhaling steam that climbs toward the cavern ceiling in slow spirals, their silhouettes backlit by the same blacklight suns that render the entire scene in photographic negative—darkness made legible through fluorescence, industrial monumentalism preserved in spectral geometry. The air itself is thick at thirty-six degrees Celsius, vibrating at ninety decibels with the combined hum of two hundred forges, a cathedral of manufacturing whose decoration has been stripped by thermal violence, leaving only function and the faint ghost of ancient craft traditions etched in heat-damaged brass.
