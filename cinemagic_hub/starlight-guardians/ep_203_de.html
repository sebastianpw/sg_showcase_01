Harbormaster Jae stands at the edge of Tier Seven's pressure gate, her fingerprint already registered on the logbook spine—the same logbook she has filled for 184 days. The harbor water beneath her moves with an unshakeable wrongness: Fluidica current bends rightward in lazy, unconscious spirals instead of the clockwise dawn schooling she has monitored since she took post. Her cornea catches the reflection of bioluminescent markers as she sketches the drainage pattern into her personal study—not the official report the Port Authority dismissed, but the real one, the one where she uses words like atavism and defeatism to describe how the water seems to remember something older than itself. As she holds the ancient records to the light, the crystals reverberantly broadcast their data: the same 0.8-degree deviation. The same counterclockwise aberration. The same wrongness. 1878 years of records, and it has happened before—but not here. Not in Tidalcross. Not until the geometric vessel docked and left its fingerprint on her water.

At the Pressure Gate threshold between Surface and Descent, Kessa Tideborn stands motionlessa figure worn by three decades of watching others fail to listen. The Surface merchant's voice still echoes off the basalt frame, needlessly rapid, each word a small violation of the Deep Folk's vernal silence. The intermediary, pale-skinned and gill-flushed indigo along their neck, does not move; their stillness is not hesitation but a form of speech Kessa has learned to read like a stethoscope reads a heartbeat. When the merchant finally slunk away in frustration, the intermediary remains, and Kessa steps forwardnot rushing, not hurryingto place both palms flat against the Gate's lower threshold, a gesture that says I am still here, I am listening with my whole body. In that narrow moment, something small and tangible passes between them: a canopic vessel, a sealed reed-scroll, perhaps a vial of ichorthe exchange soundlessly intelligently conducted. Kessa pockets the object without inspection, her jaw set in resolute calm. The Gate sighs open, its hydraulic breath releasing, and she steps through into the Descent, leaving the Surface's flamboyant noise behind her. Cool blue and teal color palette dominates the scene with muted desaturated tones. The Gate's massive hydraulic mechanism dominates the composition, the pale intermediary visible in peripheral stillness, the merchant's departing figure dissolving into echoing basalt shadows. Soft diffused lighting bathes the threshold, volumetric atmospheric haze rolling between the Surface and Descent realms.
